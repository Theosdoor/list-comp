\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

In this paper, we showed that SAEs trained on a relative-magnitude composition mechanism can learn graded latents. These latents are not just active or inactive. Their activation magnitudes correlate with $\Delta\alpha$, and steering them predictably changes the transformer's output.
% These results indicate that the activation values of SAE latents, and not only their active/inactive status, carry information that is necessary for understanding model behaviour in this setting, which is a different perspective on transformer features than is currently prevalent in the mechanistic interpretability literature.
We will release our trained SAEs on Weights \& Biases to support future work and reproducibility of our results.
Limitations are listed in Appendix \ref{app:limitations}.

In future work we will attempt to find graded latents in real-world pretrained SAEs.
The Gemma Scope releases \citep{lieberum2024gemma,mcdougall2025gemmascope2} provide a natural test environment. They include JumpReLU and Matryoshka SAEs trained on Gemma 2 and Gemma 3 at multiple widths, matching the SAE variants studied here. The target would be to find SAE latents in pretrained models where the activation value, rather than just feature presence, causally affects downstream behaviour.

% Moreover, \cite{farrell2025order} did not fully characterise the failure modes of the base transformer model (roughly 9\% of inputs), and it is worth investigating whether these relate systematically to the cases where latent steering also fails.

In summary, this paper gives a controlled example where SAE latent activation values matter for interpretation. We hope this case study motivates further work on magnitude-based feature composition in LLMs.
